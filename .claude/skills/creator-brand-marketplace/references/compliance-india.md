# Compliance awareness: India (not legal advice)

Last researched **12 Oct 2026** from secondary sources (law-firm blogs and news). Primary texts were not all retrieved. Re-verify against the regulator's own publication before shipping anything that depends on these rules, and flag the item for professional legal review in your report. **Never present these notes to users as legal advice.**

## Advertising disclosure (affects briefs, the creator workspace, and the content review flow)

- **ASCI Influencer Guidelines** (self-regulatory, in force since 14 Jun 2021): any post with a "material connection" (money, free product, discount, trip, relationship) needs an upfront, hard-to-miss label such as *Ad, Advertisement, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift*. The label must not be buried in hashtags. ([telecomtalk](https://telecomtalk.info/asci-guidelines-require-mandate-disclosure-content-disclosure/365086/))
  - *Unverified:* one law-firm post describes a **2026 ASCI update** that covers AI or virtual influencers, format-specific disclosure, and labels kept for a story's full duration. ([sansalegal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers)) Confirm on asci.org.in.
- **Department of Consumer Affairs, "Endorsement Know-hows!"** (Jan 2023, under the Consumer Protection Act 2019, enforced by the CCPA): disclose in the content itself (in the video, not only the description; on screen for the whole of a livestream), and don't endorse what you haven't used or checked. ([PIB](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/PIB1904528.pdf), [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/india/social-media/1283676/endorsement-know-how-guidelines-for-celebrities-virtual-influencers-and-influencers))
- **Product implications:** briefs should state the required disclosure label. Review checklists should include "disclosure visible". Barter (free product) still counts as a material connection.

## Finance niche (the "Finance" niche and the PaisaPal / "money-made-simple" campaigns)

- **SEBI** (in force from 29 Aug 2024, tightened by a Jan 2025 circular): SEBI-regulated entities (brokers, AMCs, RIAs, RAs, and others) may not associate with unregistered persons who give investment advice or make return claims. A narrow carve-out exists for pure education, which may not use market price data from the preceding three months. ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-bans-regulated-entities-from-associating-with-unregistered-finfluencers), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html))
- **Product implications:** a finance campaign brief could ask whether the poster is a SEBI-regulated entity. If yes, require creator registration details or an education-only scope. This should be a hard eligibility field, not a soft "check with brand". Health or medical claims have similar ASCI qualification expectations; verify before building.

## Tax on barter and payments

- **Section 194R of the Income-tax Act** (from 1 Jul 2022): a business providing benefits or perquisites over ₹20,000 per financial year to a resident must deduct 10% TDS. Products that influencers keep are commonly treated as covered; products returned are not. ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/taxation/taxation-of-social-media-influencers/), [TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/section-194r-tds-benefits)) Cash fees for creator services may fall under other TDS sections (sources differ). GST registration may apply to creators above the threshold.
- **Product implications:** barter listings show "Worth ₹X". A tooltip or FAQ can note that retained products may have tax implications, without giving advice. Real payouts will need PAN/GST capture, TDS handling, and invoices, all of which require a CA's review.

## Personal data: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

- The Rules were notified in **Nov 2025** with phased commencement: the Data Protection Board provisions apply immediately, consent-manager provisions apply about 12 months later (around **Nov 2026**), and the core obligations (notice, verifiable consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, and so on) apply about 18 months later (around **May 2027**). ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fcentre-notifies-dpdp-rules-implementation-planned-in-phases-spread-over-12-18-months-3798465), [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-))
- **Product implications:** any real backend needs purpose-specific notices, unbundled consent, account deletion and data export, retention limits, breach response, and verifiable parental consent for under-18 users. Many creators are minors, so age-gate sign-up. Audience demographics are aggregate data; don't collect follower-level personal data.

## Contracts, IP, platform safety

- Creators keep copyright unless a signed agreement transfers or licenses it. The current Terms draft says the same. Usage rights should have a duration, channels, a paid-ads flag, and exclusivity, all explicit and accepted by both sides.
- Cancellation, kill fees, refunds, and dispute steps must be visible before hire once real payments exist.
- Keep moderation reasons, reports, and admin actions in an auditable log once a backend exists. Don't auto-ban or auto-label someone as fraudulent.
- Contact masking until hire protects both sides and helps keep deals on-platform. Don't remove it.

## When to escalate to legal review

Final Terms and Privacy text, payment, escrow, payout, or fee flows, contract templates, finance or health campaign rules, handling of minors, cross-border creators, and any claim like "protected", "guaranteed", "compliant", or "safe payments".
